Horizon: plans in projects

A plan is a project's steps in order, each with its own date, made all at once as real cards. Every step is on the board from day one. The plan lives in a new Plan panel on the project page, above Notes and Links; it reads like the chain in What you did, and changes like the checklist.

Drawn with the site's own styles. The project, steps, and dates are made up; "today" is Sun, Nov 22.

Nothing on this sheet saves or moves. It's for your eye.

A

The project page, with a plan

The Plan panel sits first in the side column. Under its title, one line: how far along, and what's next. Then the steps, oldest first, in the chain's own look: a filled check for done, an open circle for still on the board, a dash for let go. The next open step is in bold. Each step is a button: an open one opens its card's editor, a finished one opens the box What you did uses.

The steps are also cards, so they sit in the groups on the left like any of the project's cards, at their own dates. The left says when; the plan says in what order. Cards carry no plan mark on their face (settled).

Out of order: step 5 is dated before step 4 (you moved it on the board). Nothing moves for you; the step wears a soft yellow "Before step 4's date", the same yellow the Inbox uses for a date worth a second look.

B

Making a plan

No plan yet: the panel says what a plan is, with one button. Make a plan turns it into the checklist's add field: type a step, Enter, and its row appears with the date box ready; Enter again goes back to the add field for the next one. A handle on each row drags it (Alt and the up or down arrow from the keyboard, as in the checklist); the X drops a row that isn't made yet.

Category: every card needs one, so the steps get one together at the top (each can be changed later in its editor). An empty date is allowed: that step goes to Someday, as any card without a date does. Add 6 steps to the board makes them all in one write.

C

Changing a plan later (questions 1, 2, 3)

Change turns the plan into the same rows as making one. Steps already done or let go keep their place, locked, with a check. Open steps can be renamed, redated, dragged into a new order, or taken off.

Adding (question 1): one way in, the add field at the bottom. A new step lands at the end; to put it between two steps, drag it up. That answers "between" without a second control on every row.

Taking off (question 2): the X takes a step off the plan, not off the board. It stays a card in this project, at its date. The row stays struck through with Undo until you Save. Let it go stays a separate act, where it is now (triage).

Reordering (question 3): the handle, as in the checklist. Order and dates stay independent: dragging a step doesn't change its date, and the yellow note says when they disagree. Save writes every change in one write; Cancel leaves the plan as it was.

D

A step's editor (questions 4 and 6)

The line on top (question 6): a follow-up looks back at what made it: "After Weed graphic novels, done Tue, Nov 24." A plan step says where it stands: "Step 3 of 6, after Confirm the lion dance troupe, due Mon, Nov 30" (or "done Thu, Nov 19" once that one's done). A different icon (a numbered list, not the follow-up's bent arrow), and the step number first, so the two never read alike.

Dates: when Due is earlier than the step before it, the yellow note says so under Due. Nothing is moved.

Repeats and Follow-up (question 4): Repeats is off and grayed for a plan step, with a note why. Follow-up works, and its card joins the plan: made when this step is done, it becomes the next step, and the steps after it shift down one. Its box says so.

On the board and in What you did

The hover box adds one line: "Step 3 of 6 in the plan." Mark done on a step uses the line beside quick add that follow-ups already use, to say what's next in the plan (my addition: say no and it goes). What you did: a step's box shows "The plan" instead of "The chain", numbered, this step in bold.
