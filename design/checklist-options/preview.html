Horizon: checklists on cards

Three things to choose: what a card on the board shows (A to D), how the editor's checklist moves its items (E or F), and whether What you did and the hover box show the items (G). Each card is drawn at a real zone's width: Today's, about 300 pixels, and Later's, about 200.

The editors work: tick a box, type in "Add an item" and press Enter, change an item's words, remove one. In E the arrows move an item; in F, Alt and the up or down arrow moves the item you're typing in (the handle is for dragging, which this sheet doesn't do). Tick every box to see the offer to mark the card done.

On the board

A

The count

My recommendation

A small checkbox and "2/5" in the meta row, in the mono type every count and date uses. When every item is ticked the box fills and the count turns blue: the shape changes, not only the color. It costs no height, so the board stays a board.

A screen reader hears Checklist, 2 of 5 ticked.

B

The count and a thin bar

A's count, plus a 4-pixel bar under the meta row that fills in blue. Easier to read from across the room; the risk is that it rhymes with the category band at the card's top, and that a board of bars starts to feel like a progress report.

A screen reader hears Checklist, 2 of 5 ticked.

C

The items themselves

The first three unticked items under a dashed line, then "+2 more". The most to read at a glance, and the most height: a board of these gets long fast, and folds sooner. Read only: if you later want to tick on the board, each line would need a 44-pixel target, and the cards would double in height.

A screen reader hears the list: Checklist, 2 of 5 ticked, list, 3 items.

D

A mark, nothing else

A checklist mark at the top right, beside the notes mark. The calmest; but you can't tell 0/5 from 5/5 without opening the card, which is most of what a count is for.

A screen reader hears Has a checklist.

In the card editor

Both put the checklist right under Notes, where content lives, before the date and the fields about the card. Ticked items stay where they are, struck through. Ticking saves at once (question 1 in PLAN.md); adding, rewording, removing, and moving save with Save, like everything else in the editor.

E

Up and down arrows, as in Settings' categories

Each row: the box, the words, an up arrow, a down arrow, and an X. Nothing new to learn, and no dragging code. The cost is room: at the editor's width the words get about 190 pixels, so longer items wrap to two or three lines, and moving the last of ten items to the top is nine taps.

F

A handle to drag, Alt and arrows on the keyboard

My recommendation

Each row: a six-dot handle, the box, the words, and an X. Drag by the handle (mouse or finger) to move an item; with the keyboard, Alt and the up or down arrow moves the item you're typing in. The words get about 250 pixels. The cost: new dragging code for the list (a small cousin of the board's), which is the biggest single piece of this slice.

After it's done, and on hover

G

What you did, Copy as text, and notes on hover

My recommendation

What you did's rows stay as they are. Opening a row's box shows the checklist under the notes, every item with its tick (left unticked items included: the record stays honest). Copy as text with "Include notes" also lists the items, [x] for ticked and [ ] for not. And resting the pointer on a card shows its items under its notes, read only, even on a card with no notes.
